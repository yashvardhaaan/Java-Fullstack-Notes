# Assembly

## Definition

**Assembly** is a spatial reasoning topic in which separate pieces or components are combined to form a **complete figure or object**.

The objective is to determine **which parts fit together**, **the correct orientation**, or **the final assembled shape**.

General Structure

```
Individual Pieces

        ↓
 Rotate (if needed)

        ↓
 Match Edges

        ↓
 Assemble

        ↓
 Complete Figure
```

Example

```
Pieces

┌─┐   ┌─┐
│ │ + │ │
└─┘   └─┘

↓

Combined

┌─────┐
│     │
└─────┘
```

---

# Why Learn This?

Assembly develops visualization and spatial reasoning skills.

Applications include:

- Mechanical Engineering – Assembling machine components.
- Manufacturing – Product assembly lines.
- Robotics – Object assembly and manipulation.
- CAD Software – Building 3D models from parts.
- Construction – Structural component assembly.
- Product Design – Combining multiple parts into a final product.

# Important Rules / Properties

### Property 1

Every piece is used exactly once unless stated otherwise.

---

### Property 2

Pieces cannot overlap.

---

### Property 3

Edges should align perfectly.

---

### Property 4

Rotation is allowed only if specified or visually possible.

---

### Property 5

Reflection (flipping) is **not** allowed unless explicitly mentioned.

---

# Solving Strategy

```
Observe All Pieces
         │
         ▼
Identify Unique Edges
         │
         ▼
Rotate if Required
         │
         ▼
Match Adjacent Edges
         │
         ▼
Verify Complete Figure
```

---

# Shortcuts & Tricks

### 1. Find Corner Pieces First

Corner pieces usually have two outer edges.

---

### 2. Match Unique Shapes

Look for uncommon notches, curves, or angles.

---

### 3. Assemble the Boundary First

Build the outer frame before filling the center.

---

### 4. Eliminate Impossible Fits

Reject pieces that create overlaps or gaps.

---

### 5. Check Orientation Carefully

Many incorrect answers differ only by rotation.

# GATE 80/20 Summary

✅ Begin with **corner and boundary pieces**.

✅ Match **unique edges, curves, and notches** first.

✅ Rotate pieces when necessary, but avoid flipping them unless the question explicitly allows reflection.

✅ A correct assembly has:

- No overlaps
- No gaps
- Every piece used exactly once
- Correct orientation

✅ Most Assembly questions can be solved efficiently by eliminating impossible edge combinations before trying every arrangement.
