# Simple and Compound Interest

## Definition

**Interest** is the extra amount paid or earned for borrowing or investing money over a period of time.

There are two main types of interest:

- **Simple Interest (SI)** – Interest is calculated only on the original principal.
- **Compound Interest (CI)** – Interest is calculated on both the principal and the accumulated interest.

---

# Why Learn This?

Interest calculations are essential for understanding loans, savings, investments, and financial planning.

Applications include:

- **Banking** – Savings accounts, fixed deposits, and recurring deposits.
- **Finance** – Home loans, car loans, education loans, and investments.
- **Business** – Investment analysis and capital growth.
- **Accounting** – Financial statements and interest calculations.
- **Personal Finance** – EMI planning, savings, and retirement planning.
- **Economics** – Inflation, lending, and borrowing analysis.

# Basic Terminology

## Principal (P)

The original amount borrowed or invested.

Example

```
Principal

=$10,000
```

---

## Rate of Interest (R)

The percentage charged or earned per year.

Example

```
Rate

=8% per annum
```

---

## Time (T)

The duration for which the money is invested or borrowed.

Example

```
Time

=5 years
```

---

## Amount (A)

The total money after adding interest.

```
Amount

=

Principal + Interest
```

---

# Simple Interest (SI)

Simple Interest is calculated only on the **Principal**.

Formula

```
SI

=

(P×R×T)/100
```

---

# Amount in Simple Interest

Formula

```
Amount

=

Principal + SI
```

or

```
A

=

P(1+RT/100)
```

---

# Example (Simple Interest)

```
P

=$5,000

R

=10%

T

=3 years
```

Interest

```
SI

=(5000×10×3)/100

=$1,500
```

Amount

```
5000+1500

=$6,500
```

---

# Compound Interest (CI)

Compound Interest is calculated on the **Principal + Previously Earned Interest**.

Interest is added to the principal after every compounding period.

---

# Compound Amount Formula

### Annual Compounding

```
A

=P(1+R/100)^T
```

---

# Compound Interest Formula

```
CI

=A−P
```

---

# Example (Compound Interest)

```
P

=$10,000

R

=10%

T

=2 years
```

Amount

```
10000(1.10)^2

=10000×1.21

=$12,100
```

Compound Interest

```
12100−10000

=$2,100
```

---

# Difference Between SI and CI

| Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Calculated only on Principal | Calculated on Principal + Interest |
| Growth is Linear | Growth is Exponential |
| Lower Returns | Higher Returns |
| Easier Calculation | Slightly More Complex |

Amount−Principal

### 4. Adjust Rate and Time

For half-yearly or quarterly compounding:

# GATE 80/20 Summary

✅ Memorize the two most important formulas:
- **SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100**
- **CI = P(1 + R/100)^T − P**

✅ Always identify whether the question asks for **Simple Interest** or **Compound Interest**.

✅ Adjust the **rate and number of periods** for half-yearly, quarterly, or monthly compounding.

✅ Remember that **Compound Interest grows exponentially**, while **Simple Interest grows linearly**.

✅ Most aptitude questions involve **interest, amount, depreciation, investment growth, and the difference between SI and CI**.
